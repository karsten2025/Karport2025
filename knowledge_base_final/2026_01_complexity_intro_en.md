# A Complexity Primer — Introduction

## Overview
This entry synthesizes practitioner-oriented guidance about complexity science: what complexity is, why it matters, and how to recognize it in real-world systems. It is written as an original paraphrase (no verbatim copyrighted text) to ensure legal safety and high usability for the chatbot.

## Core definition and framing
- Complexity arises when many heterogeneous, interacting elements produce macro-level behaviors that cannot be deduced from single parts alone.
- Key ingredients: diversity, connectivity, interdependence and adaptation.
- System classes:
  - Simple: predictable, few interacting parts.
  - Complicated: many parts but decomposable and predictable when analyzed.
  - Complex: adaptive, emergent, and sensitive to small changes.

## Practical diagnosis
- Quick checks: Do agents adapt? Do interactions create unexpected macro patterns? Are outcomes sensitive to small changes or nonstationary? If yes, apply complexity-aware methods.

## Recommended next steps
1. Classify the system (problem/solution/environment) with short heuristics.
2. Choose modeling and intervention approaches aligned to that classification.
3. Favor prototyping, monitoring and iterative governance.
